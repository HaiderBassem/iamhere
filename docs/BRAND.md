# I Am Here — Brand brief

A first synthesis in the shape of the ECC `brand-discovery` brandbook (purpose, positioning, audience, personality,
voice, narrative). It is derived from the business concept, not from founder interviews. Items marked
**(assumption)** should be confirmed or corrected in a short interview with the founders; the visual identity is in
[`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md).

## Purpose (why)

People show up. Their showing up should count, without a queue, a paper sheet or a friend signing for them.
I Am Here exists so that **being there is enough**: the organizer gets a list they can trust, and the attendee
spends one second on it.

## Positioning

| | |
|---|---|
| **Competitive alternatives** | Paper sheets; calling names; a Google Form behind a static QR code; card or fingerprint terminals |
| **Unique attributes** | A code that changes every 5 seconds; no app; one account everywhere; location and room checks that only ask for more when it matters |
| **Value** | Minutes back in every session, and an attendance list that reflects who was really in the room |
| **Target** | Organizers who take attendance weekly: universities and schools first, then training centres, companies and events. Iraq first **(assumption: Erbil/Baghdad universities as launch customers)** |
| **Category** | Attendance, not "event management" and not "HR software". Narrow on purpose |

**One line:** *I Am Here takes attendance in one scan, and makes sure the people on the list were in the room.*

## Audience

- **The organizer** (the buyer's user): a lecturer with 60 students and no assistant. Wants to start in seconds and never think about it again. Fears a tool that slows the class down in front of everyone.
- **The attendee:** a student or employee at a door with 20 seconds and 12% battery. Wants it to just work, and to be counted.
- **The institution** (the buyer): a registrar, dean or HR manager. Wants reliable reports, privacy and control. Fears a fraud scandal or a data leak.

## Personality

Archetype: **the Everyman with a Sage's backbone** **(assumption)**. Ordinary, friendly and for everyone, but exact
and trustworthy where it counts. On Aaker's dimensions it is high on *sincerity* and *competence*, low on
*excitement* and *ruggedness*.

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| Calm, plain-spoken | Cute, jokey, emoji-heavy |
| Exact about what it checked | Vague ("smart AI security") |
| Fair: it assumes good faith, and the organizer has the last word | Suspicious or accusing |
| Quick | Rushed or pushy |

If the brand walked into a room, it would be the person at the door who knows everyone's name, smiles, and
still notices who is not supposed to be there.

## Voice and tone

- **Say what happens, in the person's words.** "You're checked in." Not "Attendance record created successfully."
- **Short sentences. Verbs first on buttons.** "Start attendance", "Scan to check in", "Take the photo again".
- **Never blame.** "That code has changed. Scan the screen again." Not "Invalid QR code".
- **Be exact about trust.** "Sent to your organizer to review: the photo did not show the current code." Not "Verification failed".
- **No jargon.** Not "session ID", "GPS anchor" or "visual verification". Say "this session", "the place" and "a photo of the screen".
- **Tone shifts with stakes:** cheerful on success (one word), steady on problems (what happened plus the next step), formal only on legal pages.

## Narrative (story arc)

Before: a sheet goes around the room for ten minutes, and a third of the signatures are friends' handwriting.
Then: the lecturer types the class name, and a code appears on the screen. Three seconds later the first names
arrive, and fifty arrive in a minute. Nobody outside the room can get on the list, and the registrar gets the
report without asking. **One title. One QR code. Everyone checked in.**

## Naming and copy rules

- The product is **I Am Here** (three words, each capitalised); never "IAmHere" or "IAH" in the interface.
- An **attendance session** is "a session" in the interface. An **organizer** is "organizer", an **attendee** is "attendee".
- Modes are **Normal** and **Strict**. Strict is described by what it checks ("location + a photo of the screen"), never as "AI".
- Numbers in the interface use the reader's locale (Arabic-Indic digits in Arabic, when Arabic ships).

## Open questions for the founders

1. Is Iraq-first the launch, with English as the first interface language, or should Arabic ship first?
2. Is the brand an independent company, or part of a larger group (Skillture)? If so, should it show "by Skillture"?
3. Which institutions are the first pilots? Their colours or logos may appear in co-branded reports later.
